This guide is general information about this operation. Always follow the instructions you receive from your surgeon and the hospital. Where they differ from this page, their instructions apply.

About the operation

A craniotomy is brain surgery in which the surgeon removes part of the skull to reach the brain. It is used to treat brain tumours, vascular malformations, skull fractures and blood clots.

Craniotomy or craniectomy?

In a craniotomy, the piece of skull is removed and then put back at the end of the operation. In a craniectomy, the piece of skull is not replaced right away. A later operation, called a cranioplasty, may be needed to replace it.

How long it takes

The length of surgery varies from case to case, typically 2 to 4 hours.

Before surgery

Booking

Your surgeon will discuss your diagnosis with you, arrange any tests needed, review your scans and confirm this is the right approach. Once you consent, you are added to the wait list and our office will call you with a date. Your pre-operative appointment is booked at the same time.

Pre-operative appointment

Once your surgery is booked, you will also be booked for an appointment at the Pre-Operative Assessment Clinic (POAC). This is usually at Queensway Health Centre, 150 Sherway Drive, Etobicoke, and sometimes at Credit Valley Hospital. Your booking details will confirm the location. Parking and directions.

Eating and drinking

For your safety, your stomach must be empty before surgery. If these instructions are not followed, your surgery will be rescheduled.

  • Solid food: you may eat normally until midnight the night before your surgery.
  • Clear fluids: you may drink up to 500 mL (2 cups) of clear fluids until 3 hours before your surgery time. Clear fluids are water, ginger ale and apple juice.

Medications

Unless your surgeon tells you otherwise, stop anti-inflammatory medications (such as Advil or aspirin) and all blood thinners 5 days before surgery. If you take a blood thinner for a heart stent, a mechanical heart valve or a recent blood clot, speak with the doctor who prescribed it before you stop.

Risks

Possible risks and complications include bleeding, a blood collection (hematoma), seizures, stroke, coma, hydrocephalus and infection.

Recovery

After surgery you will be cared for in the intensive care unit or on the neurosurgical floor until you are ready to recover safely at home. Most patients spend around one week in hospital. The average recovery time is 6 to 8 weeks, and you will typically need about 6 weeks off work. Avoid physically demanding activity until your care team says it is safe.

Activity

  • Take frequent, short walks through the day if you are able, using any recommended mobility aids
  • You may be referred for rehabilitation. For in-home therapy, Ontario Health atHome will usually contact you a few days after discharge
  • Do not drive until you are back to your usual self or your doctor clears you, typically 2 to 6 weeks. If you had a seizure, you may not drive until you have been seizure-free for at least 6 months and cleared by a doctor
  • Avoid heavy work, heavy lifting or prolonged bending for 4 to 6 weeks
  • Avoid activities that could injure your head, such as contact sports

Wound care

  • You will likely still have staples or stitches when you go home
  • You may leave the incision open to the air 3 to 4 days after surgery. Keep it clean and avoid touching it. If that is difficult, cover it with a dressing and change it daily
  • You can start showering 3 to 5 days after surgery. Wash daily with a gentle, unscented soap or baby shampoo. Do not scrub the incision or submerge it in water until it has fully healed, usually about 6 weeks. Pat it dry
  • Do not apply oils, lotions, creams or hair products near the incision until it has healed, usually 4 to 8 weeks
  • See your family doctor to have the staples removed about 2 weeks after surgery. If you do not have a family doctor, a walk-in clinic can do this

When to get help

If you notice increasing redness, pain or drainage from the incision, contact your family doctor or surgeon.

Seek immediate medical attention or return to the emergency department for signs of infection (fever, pus-like fluid from the incision, or an incision that looks red and swollen), clear fluid leaking from the incision, the incision reopening, or a seizure.

Follow-up

You will have a follow-up appointment at our clinic during your recovery. If you have not been given a date, or you have concerns, call (905) 291-4506.
