Posterior cervical decompression and fusion (PCDF)

An operation performed through the back of the neck to relieve pressure on the spinal cord or nerves, and to stabilize the cervical spine.

Hospital stay
Usually 3 to 5 days
Anesthesia
General
Time off work
At least 6 weeks
Follow-up
4 to 6 weeks

This guide is general information about this operation. Always follow the instructions you receive from your surgeon and the hospital. Where they differ from this page, their instructions apply.

About the operation

A posterior cervical decompression and fusion is performed through an incision at the back of the neck. The decompression creates more space for the spinal cord or nerves by removing bone or other tissue that is causing pressure. The fusion stabilizes the affected vertebrae. Screws and rods are commonly used to hold the cervical spine in position while bone graft encourages the vertebrae to join together.

Why it is done

This operation may be recommended when there is pressure on the spinal cord or nerve roots and the affected part of the cervical spine also needs to be stabilized. Reasons include:

  • Cervical spinal stenosis
  • Cervical myelopathy (spinal cord compression)
  • Cervical radiculopathy (nerve root compression)
  • Degenerative changes in the cervical spine

What it is intended to treat

Depending on the cause, surgery may aim to prevent further neurological decline, improve symptoms, or both. Symptoms can include neck pain, arm pain, numbness or tingling in the arms or hands, weakness, and loss of hand coordination.

Before surgery

Booking

You will meet with your surgeon to go over your medical history and MRI and confirm this is the right operation for you. Once you consent, you are added to the wait list and our office will call you with a date, usually within 2 to 3 weeks. Your pre-operative appointment and any further imaging are booked at the same time.

Pre-operative appointment

Once your surgery is booked, you will also be booked for an appointment at the Pre-Operative Assessment Clinic (POAC). This is usually at Queensway Health Centre, 150 Sherway Drive, Etobicoke, and sometimes at Credit Valley Hospital. Your booking details will confirm the location. Parking and directions.

Eating and drinking

For your safety, your stomach must be empty before surgery. If these instructions are not followed, your surgery will be rescheduled.

  • Solid food: you may eat normally until midnight the night before your surgery.
  • Clear fluids: you may drink up to 500 mL (2 cups) of clear fluids until 3 hours before your surgery time. Clear fluids are water, ginger ale and apple juice.

Medications

Unless your surgeon tells you otherwise, stop anti-inflammatory medications (such as Advil or aspirin) and all blood thinners 5 days before surgery. If you take a blood thinner for a heart stent, a mechanical heart valve or a recent blood clot, speak with the doctor who prescribed it before you stop.

Risks

As with most surgery, there is a risk of infection and bleeding. Other complications can include nerve injury and injury to the neck muscles. Results vary from person to person, and surgery may not relieve all of your pain.

Recovery

Length of stay varies, but patients usually remain in hospital for 3 to 5 days, until pain is controlled and they can move around safely. Plan to take at least 6 weeks off work.

Activity restrictions

Weeks 1 to 4: avoid bending at the waist, twisting your body, lifting anything over 5 lb, and driving, especially while taking opioid pain medication.

Weeks 4 to 8: continue to avoid bending and twisting, and lifting anything over 10 lb. Avoid long car rides or take frequent breaks.

Your surgeon will review your lifting restrictions at your follow-up visit.

Wound care

  • Change your dressing daily until your staples are removed
  • Gently clean the incision with saline or tap water, using sterile gauze or a clean washcloth. Start in the middle and work outward
  • Make sure the incision is fully dry, patting it with a clean towel or gauze, before applying a new dressing
  • You may shower 4 days after surgery. Do not soak or scrub the incision, and keep scented or abrasive soaps away from it
  • Have your staples removed by your family doctor about 2 weeks after surgery. Bring the staple remover you were given at discharge, and your discharge paperwork

When to get help

Contact your surgeon or family doctor if you notice increasing redness, pain or drainage from the incision.

Return to the emergency department for fever or chills, pus draining from the incision, worsening drainage, redness or swelling, or signs that the wound is reopening. Also seek urgent care for severe or worsening pain in your back, arms or legs, new numbness, tingling or weakness, or new problems controlling your bladder or bowels.

Follow-up

Your follow-up appointment with your surgeon is about 4 to 6 weeks after surgery, at our clinic at 101 Queensway West. If you have not been given a date, or you have concerns, call (905) 291-4506.
