Procedure guide · Spine

Anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF)

An operation performed through the front of the neck to relieve pressure on the spinal cord and nerve roots, and to stabilize the affected part of the cervical spine.

Hospital stay
Usually same day
Operation
2 to 3 hours
Time off work
At least 4 to 6 weeks
Follow-up
4 to 6 weeks

This guide is general information about this operation. Always follow the instructions you receive from your surgeon and the hospital. Where they differ from this page, their instructions apply.

About the operation

An ACDF is performed through a small incision in the front of the neck. It removes pressure from the spinal cord and nerve roots, and stabilizes the affected levels of the cervical spine. It is considered a major operation.

Why it is done

Surgery is recommended when non-surgical treatment has not helped. Symptoms can include:

  • Pain spreading down the arms
  • Tingling or numbness in the hands and fingers
  • Weakness in the hands and arms
  • Severe neck pain

How it is done

Your surgeon removes the affected disc and any bone spurs. The space where the disc was is then filled with bone graft or a spacer, which allows the vertebrae to fuse. The operation typically takes 2 to 3 hours, although every case is different.

Before surgery

Booking

At your consultation, your surgeon will examine you and review your imaging to confirm the operation is right for you. A new MRI is requested if needed. Once you consent, you are added to the wait list and our office will call you with a surgery date, usually within 2 to 3 weeks.

Pre-operative appointment

Once your surgery is booked, you will also be booked for an appointment at the Pre-Operative Assessment Clinic (POAC). This is usually at Queensway Health Centre, 150 Sherway Drive, Etobicoke, and sometimes at Credit Valley Hospital. Your booking details will confirm the location. Parking and directions.

Eating and drinking

For your safety, your stomach must be empty before surgery. If these instructions are not followed, your surgery will be rescheduled.

  • Solid food: you may eat normally until midnight the night before your surgery.
  • Clear fluids: you may drink up to 500 mL (2 cups) of clear fluids until 3 hours before your surgery time. Clear fluids are water, ginger ale and apple juice.

Medications

Unless your surgeon tells you otherwise, stop anti-inflammatory medications (such as Advil or aspirin) and all blood thinners 5 days before surgery. If you take a blood thinner for a heart stent, a mechanical heart valve or a recent blood clot, speak with the doctor who prescribed it before you stop.

Risks

Like any surgery, an ACDF has potential complications. These include bleeding, infection and nerve injury.

Recovery

After surgery, nurses care for you in the recovery room, and your surgeon will go over the details and your medications with you. Most patients go home the same day. Someone must drive you home. You will receive instructions for your medications, follow-up and incision care.

Plan to take at least 4 to 6 weeks off work. You will be on pain medication for a few weeks, gradually reducing it.

Activity

  • Walk every day and gradually increase the distance
  • Do not lift anything over 10 lb until your surgeon clears you
  • No bending, twisting, pulling or reaching overhead until cleared
  • Avoid strenuous activity such as running or contact sports
  • Do not drive while taking opioid pain medication
  • Stop any activity that causes pain
  • Do not sleep on your stomach, or with a pillow that raises your head too high

Diet

Eat a light diet on the day of surgery and return to your regular diet the next day. Drink plenty of fluids.

Wound care

  • Keep the dressing clean and dry for the first 48 hours
  • After 48 hours you may shower, covering the incision with plastic. No baths, pools or hot tubs until you are cleared
  • Pat the incision dry; do not rub it
  • Remove the dressing after 3 to 4 days and leave the incision open to the air
  • The stitches under the Steri-Strips dissolve on their own
  • Do not use lotion, perfume or cologne near the incision

When to get help

Contact our office if you have:

  • A fever
  • Increasing redness or drainage from the incision
  • New or worsening arm pain, numbness or weakness

Call 911 if you have swelling in your neck with difficulty breathing or swallowing. Go to the nearest emergency department if you lose control of your bladder or bowels.

Follow-up

Your follow-up appointment with your surgeon is about 4 to 6 weeks after surgery, at our clinic at 101 Queensway West. If you have not been given a date, or you have concerns, call (905) 291-4506.
