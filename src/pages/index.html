Spine

Cervical · Thoracic · Lumbar

Explore

Conditions of the spine that cause neck or back pain, pain spreading into the arms or legs, numbness, weakness or difficulty walking. Most people improve with time and non-surgical care. Surgery is considered when symptoms persist or when the nerves or spinal cord are at risk.

ConditionCommon surgical options
Lumbar spinal stenosis
Decompression (laminectomy or laminotomy), with fusion if the spine is unstable
Lumbar disc herniation
Microdiscectomy
Cervical radiculopathy
Anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) or posterior foraminotomy
Degenerative cervical myelopathy
ACDF, corpectomy, laminoplasty, or laminectomy and fusion, depending on the levels involved and alignment
Spondylolisthesis
Decompression, with or without fusion
Spinal fractures
Observation, bracing or surgical stabilization, depending on stability

Neuro-oncology

Brain and spinal tumours

Explore

Assessment and surgery for tumours of the brain and spine. Treatment is planned with the oncology team at the Carlo Fidani Regional Cancer Centre, so that surgery, radiation and systemic therapy are coordinated.

ConditionCommon treatment options
Meningioma
Observation with serial imaging, surgical resection or stereotactic radiosurgery
Glioma
Biopsy or maximal safe resection, followed by radiation and chemotherapy as indicated
Brain metastases
Surgical resection, stereotactic radiosurgery or both
Intradural spinal tumours
Microsurgical resection
Spinal metastases
Decompression and stabilization, usually combined with radiation

03

Cerebrovascular

Stroke · Aneurysm · Carotid

Explore

Endovascular (catheter-based) treatment of conditions affecting the blood vessels of the brain and neck, provided by a neurosurgeon with fellowship training in endovascular neurosurgery.

ConditionCommon treatment options
Acute ischemic stroke
Endovascular thrombectomy, an emergency treatment accessed through 911 and the emergency department
Brain aneurysm
Surveillance imaging, coiling (with balloon or stent assistance) or flow diversion
Carotid stenosis
Medical therapy, with carotid stenting for selected patients
AVM and dural AV fistula
Observation, embolization, surgery or radiosurgery, often combined
Pulsatile tinnitus and IIH
Venous sinus stenting when a venous narrowing is the cause

For patients

Before and after surgery

How a referral becomes an appointment, and how to prepare if you are having surgery.

  1. Referral

    Your family doctor, nurse practitioner, specialist or emergency physician sends us a referral with your imaging.

  2. We call you

    Our office reviews the referral and calls you to book a consultation.

  3. Consultation

    You meet your surgeon at our clinic to go over your imaging, your symptoms and your options, including treatment without surgery.

  4. Treatment and follow-up

    If you have surgery, it takes place at Mississauga Hospital. Follow-up visits are at our clinic.
