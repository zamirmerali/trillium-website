Laminectomy

This guide is general information about this operation. Always follow the instructions you receive from your surgeon and the hospital. Where they differ from this page, their instructions apply.

About the operation

A laminectomy removes part or all of the lamina, the back portion of a vertebra, to relieve pressure on the spinal cord or nerves. It is often done to treat spinal stenosis or a herniated disc.

Why it is done

  • Leg or arm pain caused by nerve compression (radiculopathy), often with back or neck pain
  • Numbness, tingling or weakness
  • Difficulty walking or keeping your balance because of spinal stenosis

How it is done

Your surgeon makes an incision in the back and carefully removes the lamina to relieve pressure on the spinal nerves. Part of a herniated disc may also be removed. Sometimes a spinal fusion is performed at the same time to stabilize the spine.

Before surgery

Booking

At your consultation, your surgeon will examine you and review your imaging to confirm the operation is right for you. A new MRI is requested if needed. Once you consent, you are added to the wait list and our office will call you with a surgery date, usually within 2 to 3 weeks.

Pre-operative appointment

Once your surgery is booked, you will also be booked for an appointment at the Pre-Operative Assessment Clinic (POAC). This is usually at Queensway Health Centre, 150 Sherway Drive, Etobicoke, and sometimes at Credit Valley Hospital. Your booking details will confirm the location. Parking and directions.

Eating and drinking

For your safety, your stomach must be empty before surgery. If these instructions are not followed, your surgery will be rescheduled.

  • Solid food: you may eat normally until midnight the night before your surgery.
  • Clear fluids: you may drink up to 500 mL (2 cups) of clear fluids until 3 hours before your surgery time. Clear fluids are water, ginger ale and apple juice.

Medications

Unless your surgeon tells you otherwise, stop anti-inflammatory medications (such as Advil or aspirin) and all blood thinners 5 days before surgery. If you take a blood thinner for a heart stent, a mechanical heart valve or a recent blood clot, speak with the doctor who prescribed it before you stop.

Risks

Like all surgery, a laminectomy has risks. These include infection, bleeding, nerve injury, a spinal fluid leak, blood clots, persistent or recurrent pain, and spinal instability.

Will the pain go away completely?

Many patients have significant relief from leg or arm pain caused by nerve compression. Results vary depending on how long the nerves were compressed. Back pain may not fully resolve if it is caused by other degenerative changes.

Recovery

A laminectomy is usually a day surgery, so you go home the same day unless you are told otherwise. You can usually return to light duties within a week. Full recovery typically takes about 4 weeks, and a return to work can be discussed at that point.

For the first weeks, avoid

  • Lifting more than 10 to 15 lb
  • Bending or twisting your back
  • High-impact activities
  • Sitting or standing for long periods during the first week
  • Driving during the first week

Physiotherapy

Physiotherapy is recommended after 4 weeks to strengthen your back muscles and improve mobility.

When to get help

Contact our office if you have:

  • A fever
  • Increasing redness or drainage from the incision
  • New or worsening leg pain, numbness or weakness

Go to the nearest emergency department if you lose control of your bladder or bowels.

Follow-up

Your follow-up appointment with your surgeon is about 4 weeks after surgery, at our clinic at 101 Queensway West. If you have not been given a date, or you have concerns, call (905) 291-4506.
