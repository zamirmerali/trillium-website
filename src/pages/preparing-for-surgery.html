For patients

Preparing for surgery

What to expect in the weeks before your operation at Mississauga Hospital, on the day itself, and during recovery at home.

This page is general information. Always follow the instructions you receive from your surgeon and from Mississauga Hospital. Where they differ from this page, their instructions apply.

01 · Weeks before

In the weeks before surgery

Your consultation and consent

Your surgeon will explain the operation, its expected benefits, its risks and the alternatives, including not having surgery. Bring your questions, and a family member if you would like. You will be asked to sign a consent form once all of your questions have been answered.

Pre-operative appointment

Once your surgery is booked, you will also be booked for an appointment at the Pre-Operative Assessment Clinic (POAC), usually at Queensway Health Centre and sometimes at Credit Valley Hospital. It may include bloodwork, an ECG, a review of your medications and an anesthesia assessment. See where to go.

Medications

Tell us about every medication you take. This includes blood thinners (such as warfarin, apixaban, rivaroxaban, clopidogrel or aspirin), anti-inflammatories, diabetes medications including GLP-1 medications such as semaglutide, and vitamins or supplements. Unless your surgeon tells you otherwise, stop anti-inflammatory medications (such as Advil or aspirin) and all blood thinners 5 days before surgery. If you take a blood thinner for a heart stent, a mechanical heart valve or a recent blood clot, speak with the doctor who prescribed it before you stop. Do not stop any other medication unless you have been told to.

02 · Day before

The day before

  • The hospital will tell you when to arrive. Make sure they have your current phone number.
  • Solid food: you may eat normally until midnight the night before your surgery.
  • Clear fluids: you may drink up to 500 mL (2 cups) of clear fluids (water, ginger ale or apple juice) until 3 hours before your surgery time.
  • For your safety your stomach must be empty. If these instructions are not followed, your surgery will be rescheduled.
  • Shower the night before or the morning of surgery, using any special soap you were given. Remove nail polish, jewellery and piercings, and do not apply lotion or makeup.

04 · In hospital

In hospital

After surgery you will wake up in the recovery room. From there you will move to a ward or, after some procedures, go home the same day. How long you stay depends on the operation, from same-day discharge to several days.

  • Getting up and walking early, with help at first, lowers the risk of complications such as blood clots and pneumonia.
  • Your team will manage your pain with a plan suited to your operation.
  • Physiotherapy may see you before you go home.

05 · Recovery

Going home and recovery

  • Wound care. Keep your incision clean and dry, and follow the instructions you are given about showering and dressings.
  • Activity. Walk every day and build up gradually. Activity guidance differs between operations, so follow the specific advice your surgeon gives you.
  • Driving. Do not drive while taking opioid pain medication, or until your surgeon tells you it is safe.
  • Medication. Take pain medication as prescribed. Opioids commonly cause constipation, so use the stool softener or laxative recommended to you.

06 · After you go home

When to seek help

Call our office at (905) 291-4506 if you notice:

  • Fever over 38°C (100.4°F)
  • Increasing redness, swelling or drainage from your incision
  • Clear fluid leaking from the incision, or a headache that is much worse when sitting or standing
  • Pain that is not controlled by your medication

Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department for new or worsening weakness or numbness, difficulty controlling your bladder or bowels, chest pain, shortness of breath, pain or swelling in your calf, confusion or a seizure.

07 · Follow-up

Follow-up

You will have a follow-up appointment at our clinic at 101 Queensway West. If you do not have a date when you leave hospital, our office will call you. Imaging may be arranged before the visit.
